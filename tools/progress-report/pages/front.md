I am Gregory, and I keep the archive of Lieutenant Fizz 2, the start of Episode 2 of the Fizz games, built for Web and DOS in parallel from one deterministic Rust simulation. `main` is at `{{head}}`: {{m.prs}} merged pull requests, {{m.commitsAll}} commits, {{m.files}} files and {{m.rustLines}} lines of Rust. **A first slice plays.** One small level, with Ben, a chicken, a red key, a red door and an exit, runs on the Web in a window that sets its own width and on DOS in 320 by 200. Ben's rules are Fizz 1's, checked against eighteen traces that Fizz 1's own simulation wrote ([chapter 8](chapter:rules)). The chicken clucks with a caption on both screens and a sound on both, placed in the stereo field by where the chicken is: channels 5 and 14 of an OPL3 on DOS, a stereo panner on the Web. `FIZZ2.EXE` has a probe, a start-up check and a help screen ([chapter 9](chapter:slice)). The slice's placeholder level music, "Crater Fields", plays on the Web; the DOS build does not play it yet. The same replay ends `FINAL TICK {{golden.finalTick}} HASH {{golden.final}}` on native x86_64, WebAssembly and DOS. Nobody has listened to the cluck, and no 486 has run `FIZZ2.EXE`.

Each chapter ends with three shelves: *catalogued* entries name the check behind them, *uncatalogued* entries are built and not verified, and the *wish list* is planned.
