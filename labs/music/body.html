Lieutenant Fizz 2

Music Lab

Sketches for Episode 2’s score, every note written and played in Undertone. Each tune starts as a small picture of something in the game. Play one, tap its sections to rearrange it live, and save what you like.

How a tune is made

  1. A picture. Six ideas from the game were each drawn as an 8 by 16 grid: a beam, a row of presses, a chain of keys, crystals, a tanker, four rooms.
  2. A riff. Columns are steps in time and rows are steps of a scale. The highest cell of each column is the melody; the lowest is its shadow.
  3. A loop. A key, a tempo and a level type are chosen for the riff, and the rest of the tune is written to answer it: bass, pad, drums, and a second voice where the picture has one.
  4. A song. The game’s own song builder lays the loop out as an intro, the tune, a contrast in a related key, a breakdown, and the tune again doubled an octave up. The lab swaps the snare-roll build for one orchestral bar on the dominant and leaves out the closing fill: the last bar leads straight back into the intro, so the song loops with no seam.
  5. A band. Every voice is nudged by a few milliseconds, a few percent and a few cents, differently each time round and identically on every replay, and placed in the stereo field (or, for one tune, around eight speakers).
  6. A game. Tunes nine to fourteen began as Pathfinder. Four players were each given a room, dreamed up from one of the maze types, and walked Ben through it in sixteen steps: how high he is, what he is doing, how tense it is. Two of the paths are relays, begun by one player and finished by another. The heights are the hook; every hold is a held note; the tension shapes how hard the tune leans in.
  7. A check. Each tune is held to fourteen rules, eight of music theory and six of feel, and the Pathfinder tunes to four more: legato, truly polyphonic, a hook that returns changed, and stillness where the tension holds. The checks read notes and numbers. Nobody has listened to these as Fizz 2’s music yet; that part is yours.
