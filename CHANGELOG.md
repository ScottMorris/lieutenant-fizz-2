# Changelog

What changed in each release of Lieutenant Fizz 2, in plain language for players. It is written by hand in the release PR, and it is the source of the release text and of `CHANGES.TXT` on the floppy. The record of each merged pull request is `WORKLOG.md`.

## Unreleased

- Kicking off a wall has a word and a sound now, on the Web and on DOS: the word `kick` rises in white at Ben's chest, as in Lieutenant Fizz 1, and a short, sharp push-off plays with it (a quick upward chirp over a scuff of noise and a low thump, about a fifth of a second). The Web's `H` list has it as the last of twenty-three. The sound is new, so data files made for an earlier build are refused, on purpose. Fizz 1's four small jokes (thirty pogo bounces in a row, eight stomps, twelve wall-kicks, twenty seconds standing still) are counted now but not shown yet, since the game has no line to show them in.
- The wall behind the rooms is whitewashed boards now, like the inside of a lighthouse, in place of the bright blue ones: Ben, the ledges and the chicken stand out against it on the Web and on DOS.
- Ben no longer pokes into a wall on his right when he stands against it, on the Web and on DOS: he is drawn stopping at the wall's front edge (before, about six pixels of his back and hand were drawn inside the brick), sliding smoothly into place over the last few pixels, and nothing of him is hidden. Against a wall on his left, and for the chicken, nothing changes. Only the drawing changes; all hashes are unchanged.
- A long fall has its own sound now, on the Web and on DOS: a friendly slide whistle that slides down about two octaves in under a second, a cartoon partner for `whoa!`. It plays once when Ben has dropped five tiles or more from the highest point of his fall or jump, so a hop, a full jump, a short step down, the pogo stick and a death stay quiet, and it has no word on screen. The Web's `H` list has it as the twenty-first sound, `whistle (long fall)`. As with the soda can, the new sound is part of the number that says which game a data file belongs to, so a `.FAP` file made before this change will not load with a newer `FIZZ2.EXE`.
- A long fall has its own sound now, on the Web and on DOS: a friendly slide whistle that slides down about two octaves in under a second, a cartoon partner for `whoa!`. It plays once when Ben's feet are one tile or more below where he left the ground, so a hop, a full jump (which comes back to where it started), a drop of less than a tile, the pogo stick and a death stay quiet, and a step down of a tile or more, such as a step or jump off the stone roof of the exit gallery, whistles, and it has no word on screen. The Web's `H` list has it as the twenty-first sound, `whistle (long fall)`. As with the soda can, the new sound is part of the number that says which game a data file belongs to, so a `.FAP` file made before this change will not load with a newer `FIZZ2.EXE`.
- A long fall has its own sound now, on the Web and on DOS: a friendly slide whistle that slides down about two octaves in under a second, a cartoon partner for `whoa!`. It plays once when Ben's feet are one tile or more below where he left the ground, so a hop, any jump (even one off a ledge), a drop of less than a tile, the pogo stick and a death stay quiet, and a step off a ledge a tile or more high, such as walking off the stone roof of the exit gallery, whistles, and it has no word on screen. The Web's `H` list has it as the twenty-first sound, `whistle (long fall)`. As with the soda can, the new sound is part of the number that says which game a data file belongs to, so a `.FAP` file made before this change will not load with a newer `FIZZ2.EXE`.
- A drink for a new life, a placeholder (provisional): a small corked bottle of bubbling grape-fizz, the Fizz elixir, lies at the right-hand end of the second ledge in the left shaft, against the wall. Walking into it drinks it at once, like a snack: it is gone, Ben gains a life, the word `glug!` rises in yellow and a short rising chime plays, on the Web and on DOS. The Web's `H` list has the chime as the twenty-second sound, `life (elixir)`. If you die, the elixir comes back with the snacks. The owner is still designing the real lives, so the elixir, the word and the chime will change. Data files from before this change are refused again (the level grew an object and the sound and word lists grew), so use files from the same download.
- The heads-up box shows `Lives` where it showed `Deaths`, on the Web and on DOS, in light green. You start with 3, each death takes one away (never below 0, and there is no game over: lives are a demo of the number and not a rule yet) and the elixir gives one back. The Web's screen-reader line says "Lives 2" in place of "Deaths 1". `DEATHS` is still in the `H` panel next to a new `LIVES` line.
- Walking off a ledge has its own sound now, on the Web and on DOS: a friendly slide whistle that slides down about two octaves in half a second, a cartoon partner for `whoa!`. It plays the moment Ben leaves a surface without jumping, whatever the height, so a step off a platform or the gallery's stone roof all whistle; a jump (even one off a ledge), the pogo stick (even switched off in the air) and a death stay quiet, and it has no word on screen. The Web's `H` list has it as the twenty-first sound, `whistle (long fall)`. As with the soda can, the new sound is part of the number that says which game a data file belongs to, so a `.FAP` file made before this change will not load with a newer `FIZZ2.EXE`.
- Ben can kick off a wall, as in Lieutenant Fizz 1: in the air, touching a wall, press Jump (a fresh press) and he is thrown up at full height and away from it. Each wall allows one kick until you land, so one wall cannot be climbed and two facing walls at least three tiles apart can; there is a quarter-second wait between kicks, and the pogo stick does not kick. It works in every level, on the Web and on DOS, and a kick has no word or sound yet. The slice does not need it: the built-in test run never kicks, so its hash is still `a08501a57d509e8a`. Kicks are new events in the transcript (`kick`).
- The chicken is a little wider and turns a little earlier. Its body is now the size of its picture (a tile and three quarters wide, a tile and a half tall) where it was a bit over half a tile tall and nine tenths of a tile wide, so it stops at a wall and at the end of a ledge with none of the bird drawn inside the wall, on the Web and on DOS. It also means a short hop over the chicken is no longer enough: land on its back from higher up, or shoot it. The level's replay, and so its saved hash, changes with it.
- Dying now starts the level again, exactly as it began: a door you opened is shut again and its key is back where it first lay (before, an open door stayed open and a spent key stayed gone), as well as the snacks, the soda cans and the chicken. Nothing else about a run changes, and in the built-in test run, whose one death comes before any door opens, nothing changes at all (the hash is still `a08501a57d509e8a`). Which key opens which door is now part of the level's data rather than the game's rules, so later levels can have doors of their own. Data files made for an earlier build are refused again, on purpose, because the numbers that say which game a data file belongs to have grown; `FIZZ2.EXE` and its data files must come from the same download.
- The exit is now a doorway: stand in it on the ground and press Up to go through it and finish the level, on the Web and on DOS. Touching the exit no longer ends the level (Lieutenant Fizz 1 does end it on a touch), pressing Up in the air does nothing, and an Up you were already holding when you walked in does not count until you let go and press it again. The built-in test run now steps off the pogo stick in the doorway, waits for the chicken's cluck to finish and presses Up (1,800 steps, about 30 seconds on DOS), so the `ta-da!` comes at step 1,746 instead of 1,664 and plays to the end, and the golden hash is `843736be6fdaeb76` (it was `a08501a57d509e8a`).
- Data files made for an earlier build are refused, on purpose: the soda can, and the new layout of the level data (a level now carries its own states and the doors that follow them, and the first layout is no longer read), are now part of the number that says which game a data file belongs to, so a `.FAP` file made before this change will not load with a newer `FIZZ2.EXE`. Use files from the same download.
- Opening a door has its own sound now, on the Web and on DOS: a slow, low creak that leans up and sags back down over a scrape of wood and iron, ending in a soft latch thud, about half a second. The word on screen is still `clunk`, and bumping your head keeps the short clunk. The Web's `H` list has it as the twentieth sound, `door`.
- The soda can is in the level: Fizz 1's refill for the Fizz Blaster, which the slice had left out. Walking into one gives five more Fizz shots (no limit, as in Fizz 1), with the word `fsssht` in light cyan and a new fizzing sound (effect 17), on the Web and on DOS. One can lies on the gallery floor by the right-hand wall, where the five shots are spent, and one on the right end of the shelf; a death puts them back and restarts the blaster at five. In the rules the can is a new kind of item in the snack list (`SnackKind::Soda`) with its own event (`Event::Can`, `can <x> <y>` in the transcript); a taken can is hashed like a taken snack. The built-in test run now refills the blaster and fires again (1,757 steps, about 29 seconds on DOS), so the golden hash is `a08501a57d509e8a` (it was `e3d629fda94149ab`).
- On the Web, the music turns dull and a little quieter while the Paused or Options screen is up, and brightens again when you resume, as in Lieutenant Fizz 1 (a filter closes in about a fifth of a second and opens in about a third of one). Sound effects are not affected. DOS does not do this yet. Nobody has listened to it yet. All hashes are unchanged.
- On DOS, the music gets quieter and duller while the Paused or Options screen is up and comes back when the game resumes, as the Web game does in Lieutenant Fizz 1 (its DOS version did not). The chip has no filter, so the sound is made by raising the operators' levels: the whole music about 3 dB down, and the sharp part of the FM voices a little softer. It fades over about a fifth of a second going in and a third coming out. Effects are untouched. Nobody has listened to it yet. `FIZZ2.EXE` grows by about 3.5 KB (a new hidden `/MIXTEST` checks it); all hashes are unchanged.
- A new heads-up box on the Web and on DOS, two rows that never change size: `Fizz` and a `Score` of five yellow digits on top, `Deaths` and a key tray below. The tray has an empty socket for each kind of key the level has (red and blue here) that fills with the key when you pick it up, with a white ring for a moment, and each key has a mark besides its colour (red a dot, blue a bar) so they are not told apart by colour alone. Snacks now add to the score, Fizz 1's points (1, 2 and 5): the points show as `+N` in the place of the word `Score` for a second and the score counts up. Motion Reduced makes the score jump and shows no ring. The Fizz number has a black edge so it stays readable when it turns red at zero. The box is wider on DOS (120 by 27 pixels, was 72 by 27); a screen reader hears which key you picked up. Only the drawing changes: all hashes are unchanged.
- Every word has a sound, as in Lieutenant Fizz 1, and so does the jump: `fzzt`, `fizzled`, `bonk`, `whoa!`, `ta-da!`, the gumdrops, `clunk`, `click - no fizz`, `boing`, a jump and `crunch` (eleven new effects, written as Undertone voices with FM and pitch steps; the chicken keeps its four clucks). They come from where they happen, panned and softened like the cluck, on the Web, and on DOS through the Sound Blaster's stereo pair, the AdLib's one voice or the PC speaker, whichever the computer has. They play with the Captions setting on or off, a sound is not left out when its word is, and on DOS the newest sound cuts the one before it off, as in Fizz 1. The sounds are first drafts and nobody has listened to them yet. On the Web, the panel you open with `H` has a new list, "Hear an effect", that plays any of the nineteen by itself. `FIZZ2.EXE` grows by about 10 KB (the sounds are 6 KB of that). All hashes are unchanged.
- The jump, the pogo `boing` and the fizz shot `fzzt` sound like Lieutenant Fizz 1's own, on both the Web and DOS. On the Web they are Fizz 1's Enhanced voices unchanged (a stepped rising jump chirp, a ragged fading wobble with a bright blip, a noise burst over a sliding sine). On DOS they are Fizz 1 DOS's own data (a two-note jump, a three-note boing and a buzzing fzzt), because the downmix of the Web's voices had made the DOS pogo sound like the first, springier one. The first versions stay as alternates that no event plays, in the H panel's "Hear an effect" list on the Web and in the DOS data: `boing (FM)`, `jump (FM)` and `fzzt (FM)`. `docs/SOUND.md` says how to switch an event to one.
- The fizzy drink can in the Paused and Options screens is purple now, the grape colour of Fizz in this game, with white bubbles, on DOS and on the Web (it was Fizz 1's red). Only the can's colours change; all hashes are unchanged.
- `FIZZ2.EXE /BENCH` also times the picture with six captions on screen and prints the extra cost; with `/AUDIO` it does the same while effects play.
- Show a word where things happen, as Lieutenant Fizz 1 does: `fzzt`, `click - no fizz`, `fizzled`, `bonk`, `whoa!`, `ta-da!`, the red, blue and green `gumdrop`, `clunk` for doors and head bumps, `boing` and `crunch`, in Fizz 1's colours, on the Web and on DOS, and hidden by the Captions setting (which still hides only the words). A repeat of the same word within a fifth of a second is left out, six words show at most, and a word far outside the picture is not shown. A new table in `fizz-slice-view` also names the sound each event uses (ids 0 to 14). The chicken's own caption and all hashes are unchanged.
- The gallery at the top of the level is now a small playground: a snack nook with cheezies, a chocolate bar, a cookie and a blue key on a shelf you reach on the pogo stick, a blue door in front of the exit, and a spot to bump your head. Taking a snack, bumping, bouncing, the empty blaster click and opening the blue door all happen in play now, each with its sound. The built-in test run is longer (1,681 steps, about 28 seconds on DOS; see the soda cans below for the length now).
