Each chicken draws a cluck timer (90 to 360 ticks) and a variant (0 to 2) from its own stream of Fizz 1's `hashf` over the tick, the chicken's index and a draw counter, so Fizz 1's trace parity is untouched and every platform clucks on the same ticks. `World::events()` returns the `Event::Cluck { variant }` values the last step emitted. - Add a fourth cluck variant, "cluck, cluck": variant 1 played twice with a 75 ms gap, the second a semitone lower and a tenth quieter, built by composing variant 1's voices with a time offset (`repeat()` in `assets/sfx/voice.ts`). `CLUCK_VARIANTS` becomes 4, so a chicken draws a variant from 0 to 3 with the same two draws per cluck; no timer moves and the final hash stays `2145503c0d3b625f`, but the slice golden's `EVENT 197 cluck 1` becomes `cluck 2`. Variant 3 adds 600 bytes of OPL2 records and 92 bytes of speaker divisors to `fizz-sfx`. Nobody has listened to it.
- Add the cluck's sound source and its DOS downmixes, with nothing audible yet. `assets/sfx/` holds the chicken's cluck as an Undertone effect with three variants (a "buh-GAWK", a short single cluck and a double cluck), a placeholder built from the project's goals that nobody has listened to. `tools/sfx/downmix.ts` converts each variant into an OPL2 chunk (channel 5, 560 Hz ticks) and a PC speaker divisor list (140 Hz) and writes the generated `crates/fizz-sfx/src/data.rs`; its `--check` runs in the CI parity job. The new `fizz-sfx` crate (`no_std`, no dependencies) adds `OplPlayer` and `SpeakerPlayer`, which step that data without allocating. The converter is adapted from fizz-dos with provenance headers. `tools/sfx/render-wav.ts` writes approximate WAV previews for listening. `docs/SFX.md` describes the pipeline and what each step loses. No manual change is needed: no build plays sound yet.
- Make `fizz-sfx` ready for OPL3 stereo effects, with nothing audible yet. An OPL record carries a bank bit (registers 0x100 and up) packed with its wait, so it is still four bytes; the cluck data stays on channel 5 in bank 0 and the plain OPL2 path's writes are pinned by a test. `OplPlayer::new_panned(variant, Pan)` enables the OPL3 (register 0x105), plays the effect on channel 14 (slot 5 of the second bank) and sets the left, right or centre output bits on its config register 0x1c5. Channels 5 and 14 are reserved for effects (`RESERVED_OPL_CHANNELS`), which costs a score one channel. `tools/sfx/render-wav.ts` also writes stereo OPL3 previews. No OPL3 detection, no Sound Blaster Pro 1 stereo, and no manual change, since no build plays sound.
- Add `fizz-machine`, the pure model of the planned DOS start-up check: the `Probe` of what a machine has with a `BLASTER` parser, the sound `Settings` with the 8-byte `FIZZ2.CFG` layout and the `/MUSIC:`, `/FX:` and shortcut flags, `resolve` from choices and probe to a sound plan with fallback reasons and a missing, available or in-use state per device row, the OPL3 write router, and `cells`, a port of Fizz 1's start-up text screen as 80 by 25 cells with a golden file of thirteen machines (`BLESS=1` regenerates it). Nothing in the DOS or Web builds uses it yet. The design is in `docs/DOS-CHECK.md`.
- Measure the slice's cost. `FIZZ2.EXE /BENCH` plays the slice script once without pacing and times the simulation step, the drawing and the copy to video memory per tick with PIT channel 0 (`timer::clocks`, 0.84 us per count, with the reading cost measured and taken off), printing averages and maxima in microseconds against the 16,667 us tick, the final hash and the usual `EXIT`, `FPU CW` and `VECTORS RESTORED` lines. `tools/check-dos.sh` runs it at `cpu_cycles = fixed 26800` (a new `DOS_CYCLES` setting in `tools/dosrun.sh`, for that run only) and asserts the line shapes and loose sanity bounds. `crates/fizz-ep2/examples/bench.rs` times the step natively. In DOSBox at 26,800 cycles a millisecond the step averages 934 us but the drawing 161 ms a tick, ten times the budget, so the drawing is the first thing to reduce for a 486. `FIZZ2.EXE` grows from 62,976 to 66,560 bytes. `docs/PLATFORM.md` gains a Performance section.
- Draw the slice about six times faster on DOS at the `/BENCH` settings (`core=normal`, `fixed 26800`): the drawing goes from 161,146 us to 24,663 us a tick and the copy to video memory from 32,441 us to 5,414 us, against the 16,667 us tick; the step, the state hash and every pixel are unchanged. The DOS runtime supplies `memset` and `memcpy` on `rep stosd` and `rep movsd`; `fizz-view` gains `draw_tilemap_on_sky`, which draws the sky and the tiles as one layer of merged runs and copies a band's first row down, and `blit_sprite` clips once per sprite; `fizz-slice-view` notes the closed doors during the tile pass and reads tile colours from a table; HUD glyphs inside the frame skip the per-pixel clip. `FIZZ2.EXE /BENCH n` times only the first `n` ticks, and `/BENCH` prints `BENCH FRAME` digests that `tools/check-dos.sh` compares with the host's (`cargo run -p fizz-present-dos --example frames`). The drawing is still 148% of a tick; `docs/PLATFORM.md` records each change, what remains, and what the DOSBox figures do not say about a 486.
